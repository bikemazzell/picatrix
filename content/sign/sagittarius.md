title: Sagittarius
source: Picatrix (adapted), Book IV chapter 2

The great sages, the chapter says, judged the Sagittarius working a rite of great power and perfection, and used it above all for finding treasure. Timed to Mercury at the fourth degree of Cancer, ten pounds of tin are cast into five dragon-shaped images; at a clear, flowing river some of the water is diverted through five small channels so that it enters each dragon's tail and leaves its mouth, filling five jugs by turns until they brim. The operator backs away twenty palms holding the jugs, pierces each with a needle, digs a trench around them, then takes river water into his mouth, returns, and spits it over jugs and dragons while silver censers of amber and aloewood smoke beside each figure. The dragons go back into the jugs and are buried before the sacrifice is made in front of a straight row of fifteen trees, the recorded words said five times to each tree. A story follows the rite: a friend given a bronze elephant holding an iron key was told that wherever the key fell from its feet, treasure lay; four cubits along, it dropped, stairs led down, and the dwelling beneath held gold, silver, and precious stones.
