title: Taurus
source: Picatrix (adapted), Book III chapter 2

For the Moon in Taurus the same watery ground serves, with a change of dress: linen dyed from dyer's weed or pomegranate rind, a hood that must not lift from the head, and washing in water boiled with nut wood. The operator jumps onto a new carpet no foot has ever trod, wrapped round in green linen with a red border, and repeats the names the text records twenty times, suffumigating with frankincense and mastic gum until the shape of a man appears. On this rite the chapter hangs a story: a friend of the author, ruined and reduced to great poverty, had Saturn for the lord of his ascendant, and Saturn was bringing the misery; shown the prayer of the Moon in Taurus, he performed the ritual, and the appearing man led him by the hand to a ditch where digging uncovered a hoard that restored his fortune. The passage also carries the doctrine that stands over the whole chapter: the Moon's effects and powers differ from sign to sign.
