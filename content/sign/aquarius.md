title: Aquarius
source: Picatrix (adapted), Book IV chapter 2

Before its rite, the Aquarius passage delivers the chapter's sternest warning: the rules of these rituals are worth more than the rituals themselves, and where theory is not joined to practice, grave danger to spirit and body follows, terrors the author says are too awful to tell; no one should approach the work without good memory and good character. The preparation is strange kitchen work: three heads of male geese cooked down in the oldest wine there is, ground in a mortar of iron or lead while the Moon is in Cancer, mixed with cinnamon, sandalwood, dried storax, incense, and goat's-thorn into forty tablets dried in a pan. At night, under the full Moon in Aquarius, fifteen censers of bronze and silver stand burning in a field, the better if golden; tablets and the sacrificed cock go onto them, and the man who appears wavers between visibility and invisibility until three more tablets and ten repetitions of the recorded words fix him wholly in view, ready for the petition.
