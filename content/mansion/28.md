title: Batn al-Hut
source: Picatrix (adapted), Book I chapter 4 and Book IV chapter 9

## Essence

The twenty-eighth mansion closes both Pisces and the whole circuit, running from about the seventeenth degree and ninth minute to the sign's last degree. Book I leaves a year-end ledger: commerce improved, cities besieged, harvests increased, things balanced, jokes ruined, treasure lost, carriage travelers protected and returned in health, peace between spouses, prisons reinforced, and sailors harmed. Book IV ends the book with fish.

## Good For

- Commerce
- Harvests, which it increases
- Balance and even measure in things
- Those traveling by carriage, returned in good health
- Peace and concord between husband and wife

## Not Good For

- A city under arms, for it besieges cities
- Jokes and merriment, which it ruins
- A man's treasure, which it works him to lose
- Captives, whose cells it reinforces
- Those sailing by ship, whom it harms

## Talisman

The Book IV image is a fish whose spine is colored, cut in zinc, the mansion's name written on its side, suffumigated with sea-fish skin, bound with string, and thrown into the water where fish are wanted; the source says the fish gather there and may be taken at will. The chapter then closes with a rule that governs every mansion image, worth ending on: the name of the mansion's lord is written on the figure together with the request, on the chest for works that join, between the shoulder blades for works that divide, and on the head for works that seek glory, honor, and standing.
